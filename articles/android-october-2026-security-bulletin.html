Android October 2026 Security Bulletin: 7 Critical Flaws, 25 CVEs

Google’s monthly AOSP patch closes 25 flaws, seven rated critical. Here’s what the bulletin confirms, what it does not, and what the “2026-10-01” patch level gets you.

Android WireShaman News Desk Oct 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Google’s October 2026 Android Security Bulletin lists 25 vulnerabilities addressed by the 2026-10-01 security patch level. Seven carry a Critical rating: six in the System component and one in Framework. The affected Android versions vary by CVE, so the table in the bulletin—not the month alone—is the authoritative scope.

The critical shortlist

The bulletin’s critical set is narrower and clearer than a speculative exploit chain:

  • System elevation of privilege: CVE-2026-55269, CVE-2026-55280, CVE-2026-58835, and CVE-2026-58880, affecting Android 16, 16 QPR2, and 17.
  • System denial of service: CVE-2026-49933, affecting Android 16 through 17, and CVE-2026-55265, also affecting Android 14 and 15.
  • Framework denial of service: CVE-2026-58865, affecting Android 14 through 17.

Google says the most severe System issue could lead to local elevation of privilege without additional execution privileges or user interaction. That is a serious outcome, but “local” still implies an attacker needs a way to execute or deliver code on the device; the bulletin does not claim that every critical issue is remotely reachable.

Why “no user interaction” is the line that matters

“No user interaction” means exploitation does not require the victim to click or approve something once the attacker reaches the vulnerable code path. It does not, by itself, mean that a flaw is remotely exploitable, wormable, or reachable by a passive network packet. Attack vector, required foothold, component exposure, and platform mitigations still matter.

The bulletin also states that Android platform and service protections reduce the likelihood of successful exploitation. No public claim in the bulletin establishes that the listed flaws form a working chain or that any of them were being exploited in the wild at publication time.

“No user interaction” removes one barrier to exploitation; it does not tell you how an attacker reaches the vulnerable component.

Samsung ships a separate vendor layer

Samsung’s October Security Maintenance Release combines Google’s Android fixes with Samsung-specific patches. Those vendor issues have their own severity scores, affected versions, and prerequisites; they should not be counted as additional AOSP criticals. For example, Samsung describes CVE-2026-21120 as a local use-after-free in the WSM service that could allow code execution with system privilege, and rates it High rather than Critical. Fleet owners should therefore check both the Android patch level and the device maker’s bulletin.

What the patch level means for your device

A device reporting security patch level 2026-10-01 or later must include the issues associated with that level and fixes from earlier bulletins. Google notes that some fixes can also arrive through Google Play system updates, while manufacturers control the schedule for full device updates. For fleet operations, record the actual patch-level string and the OEM release, rather than relying on an update label such as “October update.”

What this means for you

Verify the patch level, not the name. Check for 2026-10-01 or later and confirm the manufacturer’s corresponding release.

Do not infer a chain from a CVE list. Prioritize the update, but keep local EoP, remote DoS, and confirmed exploitation as separate risk signals.

Watch the OEM track. Pixel, Samsung, and other manufacturers can publish device-specific issues alongside the AOSP bulletin.

Keep Play Protect enabled. Google identifies platform mitigations and Play Protect as layers that reduce exploitation risk, especially for apps installed outside Google Play.
