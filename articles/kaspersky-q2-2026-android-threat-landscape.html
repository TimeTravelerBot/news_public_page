Kaspersky says Android attackers are shifting to app-store loaders as on-device malware falls

Quarterly KSN telemetry shows on-device malware declining while the most dangerous banking threats increasingly arrive as seemingly legitimate app-store apps that unpack a payload.

Android Sec ADS News Desk Aug 10, 2026 · 4 min read

Kaspersky's quarterly mobile section of its

The headline is less that mobile malware is disappearing than that it is moving where you don't look. Banking Trojans remained the largest single mobile-malware category at 30.77% of detected applications, and researchers found more than 304,000 malicious installation packages in the quarter — with 93,574 tied to mobile banking Trojans and 570 to mobile ransomware. A notable share of the increase in droppers such as Trojan-Dropper.AndroidOS.Banker and Trojan-Dropper.AndroidOS.Mamont reflects a tactical shift: banking Trojans are now frequently packed and delivered by a separate installer.

The app store is now a delivery vector

The most interesting finding in the report is that multiple malicious loaders were hosted directly on Google Play. In one instance a PDF reader app was trojanized to drop the Anatsa banking malware — it showed users a fake 'install an update' prompt that staged the banking Trojan on the device. In another, a loader inside an app (including one identified in 'Cleanova') sent telemetry and SDK-sourced install metadata back to a command-and-control server, which returned the malicious payload only for apps from certain sources. That is a deliberate way to pass app-store review: the app is benign until the server confirms it came from a distribution channel worth attacking.

  • Banking Trojans are the largest mobile-malware category (~30% of detections)
  • Dropper-based delivery (Banker, Mamont) is up, classic standalone bankers are down
  • Google Play now hosts active malicious loaders, not just accidentally-malicious apps
  • Patched vendor firmware has cleared out a wave of pre-installed Trojans
Why this matters

For consumers: if a 'PDF reader' or 'cleaner' app suddenly offers to install an update, treat it as suspicious even if it came from the Play Store. For app developers and security teams: install-source telemetry is now a real attack-signal — knowing where an app was installed is part of the exploit decision, not just an analytics detail.

What this means for you

If you're a regular mobile user: the biggest risk is no longer 'download a .apk from a shady forum' — it's install a perfectly normal app from the Play Store that quietly unpacks a banking Trojan. Check for app-store origin on any app that asks for banking permissions. If you run an Android fleet: pay attention to install-source telemetry in your EDR. An attacker choosing a payload based on distribution channel means the 'source of install' field in your telemetry is now an adversarially observed signal.
