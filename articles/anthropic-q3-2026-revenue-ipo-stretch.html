Anthropic Reports $16.8 Billion Third-Quarter Revenue as It Prepares an IPO

Reported Q3 financials — revenue of about $16.8 billion and a quarterly profit — give public-market investors a rare look at Anthropic's growth heading into a possible IPO next month.

AI & Tech WireShaman News Desk Oct 14, 2026 · 7 min read

Anthropic told pre-IPO investors its third-quarter revenue grew to about $16.8 billion, up roughly 45 percent from a second quarter of $11.5 billion, and that it turned a quarterly profit, the Financial Times reported on Wednesday. The figures and the company's reported profit were shared at a day of presentations in San Francisco, which the Wall Street Journal describes as a pitch to public-market investors ahead of a market debut as soon as next month.

The disclosure, first reported by the Financial Times and corroborated by Reuters and the Wall Street Journal, is one of the most closely watched data points in the AI industry this week. It arrives as Anthropic works toward an initial public offering and gives analysts a clearer look at the financial engine behind the Claude model family and its enterprise and API business.

The reported numbers

According to people familiar with the matter, Anthropic's third-quarter revenue reached $16.8 billion. That compares with a second quarter of about $11.5 billion and a first quarter of about $4.7 billion. The reported pace represents a steep climb: revenue roughly quadrupled between the start of the year and the end of the third quarter, and grew by roughly 45 percent in the latest quarter alone.

The Financial Times reported that Anthropic turned a profit for the period, and the Wall Street Journal separately characterizes the company as profitable in this period. That distinction matters for an IPO: a company heading toward a public listing is far more attractive to investors when it can show the revenue is scaling fast enough to also produce profit.

Why it is happening now

The revenue curve lines up with Anthropic's product cadence through the year. The company first cut the price of its Claude Haiku 5.5 models by as much as 90 percent to make cheaper, lower-latency intelligence broadly accessible, then announced a forthcoming general-purpose Claude Sonnet 5 model with a new Claude Fable 5 model that targets agentic, research, and coding work. Lowering the cost of fast inference while simultaneously offering a more capable frontier-tier model broadens the set of customers paying for Anthropic's services

It also arrives during a period of rapid movement across the broader AI sector. Google's Gemini 4 family has been rolling out in stages, OpenAI has pushed GPT-6 with an intelligent, tool-building user interface, and major cloud and chip partners continue to build out capacity for running frontier models. In that context, a reported triple-digit-growth revenue curve and a reported quarterly profit position Anthropic as one of the strongest candidates for a near-term public-market debut among the major AI companies.

How investors are framing it

Analysts and reporters are reading the figures as a test of whether Anthropic can convert its rapid growth into a durable business. As one investor cited in coverage put it, the key question is whether the company can turn its heavy demand into a sustainable model, particularly while it spends billions on training new models and running inference. The reported profit gives some answer to that question, but analysts also note the scale of capital the industry is committing to compute and data centers, which keeps pressure on the economics of serving frontier models at this level of demand.

The Wall Street Journal notes the company has been described as profitable into this period, and coverage of the pre-IPO investor day emphasizes both the revenue trajectory and the company's plans for compute and infrastructure spending over the coming decade. For a company preparing for a possible listing as soon as next month, that combination — fast reported growth, reported profitability, and a credible explanation for how it plans to sustain demand — is the core of its pitch to public-market investors.

What this means for you

What this means for you

For organizations that depend on the Claude models through API or enterprise plans, the reported revenue growth is a signal that Anthropic is scaling its capacity and, with it, the cost structure of serving its models. As the company moves toward a possible IPO, expect continued pricing moves and new model releases aimed at the enterprise market.

For investors watching the AI economy, these reported figures are the clearest look yet at whether one of the leading frontier-model companies can sustain revenue growth at this scale while also reporting a profit. The numbers reported so far point toward a stronger-than-expected position for a possible IPO, though the full picture will only be visible once the company files and prices its offering.

Keep a close eye on what Anthropic reports next. The trajectory from a first quarter of $4.7 billion to a third quarter of $16.8 billion, combined with a reported profit and a stated intent to spend tens of billions of dollars on compute, puts the company's path to a public listing firmly in focus. Whatever the eventual valuation turns out to be, this third-quarter report has substantially raised the bar for the rest of the sector.
