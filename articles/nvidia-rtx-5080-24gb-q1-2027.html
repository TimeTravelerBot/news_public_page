NVIDIA Reports a 24 GB RTX 5080 for Early 2027 as the RTX 5090 Fades From the Roadmap

A 24 GB RTX 5080 refresh is reportedly coming to Blackwell in 2027.

Hardware WireShaman Editorial Oct 12, 2026 · 6 min read

A wave of reports this week points to an unusual move from NVIDIA. According to the Taiwanese tech outlet BenchLife, the company is preparing a GeForce RTX 5080 with 24 GB of GDDR7 memory for release in the first quarter of 2027, intended to replace the current 16 GB RTX 5080. The same reports suggest the step up comes as NVIDIA winds down production of the RTX 5090, redirecting its GB202 silicon toward the professional RTX PRO line.

None of this has been confirmed by NVIDIA. No official specifications, pricing, or launch date have been disclosed, and the company has not stated that it is discontinuing the RTX 5090 or the 16 GB RTX 5080. Treat the details as well-sourced rumor rather than a product announcement.

What the reports actually claim

The reporting that surfaced on October 10 and October 12 carries three linked claims. First, a 24 GB variant of the RTX 5080 is in the pipeline for early 2027. Second, the existing RTX 5090 is being scaled back, with board partners described as no longer receiving GB202 chips for that card. Third, freed-up GB202 supply is shifting toward the RTX PRO series, which serves workstation and professional graphics buyers.

For context, the RTX 5090 launched in January 2025 with 32 GB of GDDR7, 21,760 CUDA cores, and a $1,999 suggested price. A 24 GB RTX 5080 would therefore sit below the current flagship in raw memory, which is the part that makes the story interesting: NVIDIA appears to be reshuffling the Blackwell family rather than simply adding a bigger top tier.

Why the change matters

The likely reason is practical rather than dramatic. As PC Gamer notes, the GB203 chip inside the current RTX 5080 is already well utilized, so a 24 GB refresh would not deliver meaningfully higher core counts or a big gaming jump. Its value is mostly in memory capacity, a constraint that matters more than ever in a market where larger frame buffers and heavier AI workloads keep pushing VRAM ceilings higher. That aligns with a broader industry pattern of adding memory first and raw performance later.

  • A 24 GB GDDR7 RTX 5080 is reportedly planned for Q1 2027 per BenchLife
  • NVIDIA is reportedly scaling back RTX 5090 production, with no official confirmation
  • GB202 supply is being redirected toward the RTX PRO workstation line
  • Board partners such as Asus and MSI have reportedly received little detail
  • The new card is expected to boost VRAM, not raw gaming performance

What is still uncertain

Several details remain unverified. BenchLife did not cite a public source for the Q1 2027 window, and board partners are reported to be still waiting on specifics, which suggests the design is not locked in. Analysts also differ on timing: some place a refresh around or just after CES 2027, while other leakers point to a much larger RTX 6000 generation arriving in the first half of next year. NVIDIA has said nothing, so any shopping or upgrade decision should not hinge on a single unconfirmed leak.

What this means for you

If you are shopping for a high-end GPU now, the current RTX 5090 and RTX 5080 remain valid products and their specifications have not changed. The 24 GB RTX 5080, if it ships as reported, is worth waiting for only if extra memory is the specific thing you need. For most gamers, a memory bump on a chip that is already well used is unlikely to be worth a full-year wait. Verify again once NVIDIA makes an official statement, which could change the timing, pricing, or even whether the card ships at all.
