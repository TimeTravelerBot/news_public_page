CISA Warns of Active Zero-Day Exploits in Citrix NetScaler — New CVE Added

CISA expanded its Citrix NetScaler alert on October 9, adding a new vulnerability to the list of those under active exploitation. Here is what changed and why it matters.

By WireShaman · Updated October 13, 2026 · 6 min read

CISA's alert for Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway, first published on September 27, has been updated with a new vulnerability and expanded guidance. As of October 9, the alert covers a larger set of CVEs — including CVE-2026-107406 — and urges operators to patch promptly and review Citrix's compromise-response guidance for any suspected breach. If you run a NetScaler edge device, the window between awareness and patching is where the risk lives.

What CISA said

The original alert, dated September 27, warned that critical zero-day vulnerabilities in Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway were being actively exploited. CISA's October 9 update added CVE-2026-107406 to the list of affected vulnerabilities, alongside the original set (CVE-2026-88771 through CVE-2026-88779), and clarified the potential relationship between CVE-2026-88777 and CVE-2026-88779. The alert's headline directive is unchanged: apply patches for all affected CVEs and review Citrix's guidance for suspected or confirmed compromises involving NetScaler ADC or Gateway.

CVE-2026-88779 was added to the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog on October 4. CVE-2026-107406, per the October 9 update, was incorporated into the alert as a newly confirmed exploitation target. The alert also notes that in NetScaler deployments configured as a SAML Service Provider (SP) or Identity Provider (IdP), CVE-2026-88779 can independently create a denial-of-service condition. In unpatched deployments, threat actors can chain the vulnerabilities to force a reboot and accelerate the execution of code previously injected through other exploits in the set.

Why NetScaler matters

Citrix NetScaler ADC (formerly Netscaler) is a widely deployed application delivery controller. It sits at the edge of enterprise networks — in front of web applications, identity providers, VPN gateways, and cloud services. That position makes it a high-value target: compromise a NetScaler and you can intercept, modify, or redirect traffic for every application behind it, or use it as a staging point for lateral movement inside the organization.

NetScaler deployments are also common in identity management. When a NetScaler box is configured as a SAML IdP or SP, it brokers authentication for single sign-on, enterprise portal, and cloud-directory integrations. A vulnerability in that broker can be exploited not just for network disruption but for credential capture, session hijack, or privilege escalation into downstream systems.

This is the same attack-surface pattern CISA has seen with other edge appliances: VPN concentrators, remote-access tools, and web gateways. The NetScaler variant is significant because it combines multiple independent vulnerabilities (a larger CVE set than usual for a single vendor product) with active in-the-wild exploitation confirmed by CISA.

What changed on October 9

The key material change in the October 9 update is the addition of CVE-2026-107406 to the alert. That expands the number of vulnerabilities operators must patch and the scope of devices potentially affected. The update also clarifies the relationship between two other CVEs in the set (CVE-2026-88777 and CVE-2026-88779), which matters for threat-modeling: if one CVE injects code and another forces a reboot, the combination changes the attack timeline and the detection window. (We covered the original September 27 alert last week; this article reflects the October 9 revision.)

CISA's guidance emphasizes two actions: (1) apply patches for all affected CVEs — not just the ones in the KEV catalog, but the full set cited in the alert; and (2) review Citrix's compromise-response guidance for any suspected or confirmed compromise. The second point matters because active exploitation means some systems are already compromised; patching after the fact does not undo an injection that occurred before the patch was deployed.

What this means for you

If you operate Citrix NetScaler ADC or Gateway hardware or virtual appliances, check which CVEs from the alert affect your firmware version and apply the relevant Citrix updates. If your NetScaler is configured as a SAML SP or IdP (common for enterprise SSO), CVE-2026-88779's DoS path is a separate concern even if the other CVEs are patched. If you cannot patch immediately, isolate the device or restrict access to it until the firmware update is deployed.

If you do not operate NetScaler but consume web services that route through a NetScaler, monitor for unexpected redirects, certificate changes, or authentication anomalies on those services. An actively exploited edge-device vulnerability is a signal that the upstream infrastructure has been attacked even if your application code is untouched.

Organizations with a NetScaler in their architecture should also check their incident-response playbook. CISA's emphasis on reviewing Citrix's compromise-response guidance suggests that a standard "patch and move on" workflow is insufficient — the expected threat model includes post-exploitation code that persists across reboots.

Bottom line: CISA's October 9 update expanded the Citrix NetScaler alert with a new CVE active in the wild. Patch the full CVE set, review your compromise-response posture, and treat already-deployed NetScaler devices as potentially compromised until you have validated otherwise.
